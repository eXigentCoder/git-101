AI 101: a friendly primer

What artificial intelligence is, how tools like ChatGPT work, and how to use them sensibly.

What is AI?

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the broad field of building computer systems that do things we would normally say need human intelligence: recognising images, understanding language, making recommendations or playing games.

Most of the AI you meet today is narrow: it is very good at one kind of task and has no general understanding of the world the way people do.

Machine learning

Traditional software follows rules a programmer wrote by hand. Machine learning (ML) flips that around: instead of writing the rules, we show a program lots of examples and it learns the patterns itself. A spam filter trained on thousands of labelled emails is a classic example.

Deep learning is a type of machine learning that uses large neural networks, layers of simple maths units loosely inspired by neurons, which are good at messy data like images, audio and text.

Generative AI and large language models

Generative AI creates new content (text, images, code, audio) rather than just classifying existing content. Large language models (LLMs), the engines behind chat assistants, are generative models trained on huge amounts of text.

How an LLM works (the short version)

  1. Tokens. Text is split into tokens: whole words, parts of words or punctuation. The model only ever sees numbers representing these tokens.
  2. Training. The model reads an enormous amount of text and repeatedly tries to predict the next token. Each time it is wrong, its billions of internal numbers (parameters) are nudged slightly so it does better next time.
  3. Prediction. When you send a prompt, the model predicts a likely next token, adds it to the text, and repeats, one token at a time, until the answer is complete.
  4. Tuning. Extra training on example conversations and human feedback teaches the model to follow instructions and be helpful, rather than just continue text.

An LLM doesn't look facts up in a database. It generates text that is statistically likely given everything it learned, which is both why it's so fluent and why it can be confidently wrong.

Limitations to keep in mind

  • Hallucination: it can produce plausible-sounding answers that are simply false.
  • Bias: it can repeat the biases and gaps in its training data.
  • Knowledge cut-off: it may not know about recent events unless given search or documents.
  • Context limits: it can only take a limited amount of text into account at once.

Using AI responsibly

  • Check important facts, numbers and code before you rely on them.
  • Don't paste confidential, client or personal data into tools your company hasn't approved.
  • Be open about when AI helped produce your work.
  • Keep a human in the loop for decisions that affect people.
